Music in paediatric palliative care: a new OTUT project

OTUT is beginning work on Music in Care for Ukraine. The project aims to develop practical guidance on using music, voice and gentle rhythm in the everyday support of children with serious illnesses.

Our focus is on a child’s opportunity to take part in creativity: choosing a sound, responding with a voice or gesture, and shaping shared music-making. Participation needs to reflect each child’s way of communicating, how they feel, and whether they want to join in or stop.

Why this matters

This is an important part of the discussion about quality of life in palliative care. The WHO’s definition of paediatric palliative care encompasses a child’s physical, psychological and social needs, as well as support for the family. It begins at diagnosis and can be provided alongside treatment.[1]

Collaboration between culture and health care is also developing internationally. In 2026, WHO/Europe and the EU are supporting this work in Eastern Partnership countries, including Ukraine. Their objectives include bringing together the experience of artists, health professionals and researchers, developing joint projects and evaluating their work.[2]

Where the project stands

For Music in Care for Ukraine, we have prepared a concept and begun professional discussions with international specialists. We are now working on what guidance for care workers should cover: what they can use in their everyday work, what preparation they need, and how to respond to children’s individual needs.

The next task is to develop this approach with paediatric palliative care professionals and agree on the conditions for an initial pilot.
